What you are looking at

This is the physical layout of your 8×8 weight-stationary systolic array (systolic_array.sv and pe.sv) after LibreLane took it from RTL to GDS on SkyWater's open 130 nm process. Every shape is read from the final GDS file, the same file a foundry would use to make masks. Each mask layer is raised to its real height in the SKY130 stack (approximate values, in microns). The chip is about 300 times wider than it is tall, so the Height × slider stretches it vertically; the close-up starts at true height.

The colours are false colours, one per layer, the way layout tools show them. Realistic materials switches to aluminium wiring, a gold-brown local interconnect and grey silicon.

The three scenes

  • Array: the whole 1.96 mm block. 64 identical PE macros (each one 180 µm square) sit on a 220 µm grid. Between them run the top-level wires and the skew/deskew registers. Over everything is the chip-level power grid: vertical met4 straps and wide horizontal met5 rails.
  • One PE: one processing element, every layer from the transistors to met4. This is pe.sv in silicon: 1,865 standard cells. That's 57 flip-flops, about 1,180 logic gates, about 260 buffers (the clock tree included) and 360 well taps, plus about 3,500 decap and filler cells in the gaps.
  • Close-up: a 25 µm patch of that PE's adder logic at true height. Drag Peel down to strip the layers off until the individual transistors show.

The controls

  • Hover over anything to see what layer it is, which cell sits under it, what that cell does in your design and which PE it belongs to. Click to pin the card; click again or press Esc to let it go.
  • Height × stretches the stack vertically. At 1× everything has its true proportions.
  • Peel hides every layer above the one you pick, top down. Explode pulls the layers apart vertically so you can see between them.
  • Quality: High adds ambient occlusion (soft shading in corners and gaps) and sun shadows. Ultra supersamples on top of that. Save 4K / 8K renders a PNG at that width from the current view.
  • Array detail: drawing every PE fully wired is 64 copies of the PE's metal. On a weaker GPU, "upper metals only" keeps it smooth.

The layers, bottom to top

Cell functions

A standard cell is a pre-designed gate (NAND, flip-flop, adder piece…) that is 2.72 µm tall and a multiple of 0.46 µm wide. The tools place thousands of them in rows. In Cell functions mode each cell is drawn as a coloured block. The colour shows what that cell does in your design, worked out by tracing the placed netlist:

Synthesis doesn't keep "the multiplier" and "the adder" as separate blocks. Yosys turns ps_in + a × w into one multiply-accumulate tree, so ps_in joins the same adder tree that sums the partial products. Every gate downstream of that point sees ps_in and is coloured "adder". That's why "adder" outnumbers "multiplier".

How the array works in this picture

Activations enter on the left (west) edge. The skew registers delay lane k by k cycles, then each PE passes its activation to the right through its activation register. Partial sums enter at the top, where tie cells feed constant zeros, and move down one PE per clock through the partial-sum registers. Each PE adds a × w on the way. At the bottom, the deskew registers delay column n by 7 − n cycles so all eight column sums leave together.

Things worth noticing

  • About 43% of each PE's area is decap and filler. Real logic fills only about 57% of the PE, which leaves the router room. The decaps also steady the supply.
  • met1 is mostly horizontal power rails, one pair per 2.72 µm row. Signal wiring alternates direction layer by layer: met2 vertical, met3 horizontal.
  • Each PE's logic is packed into its own 180 µm square, so its wires stay short. In the flat run the same logic was spread across the whole die. That run missed the slow corner (−3.10 ns of slack); this one passes (+3.29 ns).
  • The top level has 961 hold-fix delay cells. The clock tree delivers the clock to the registers late, so data on short paths could change before the receiving flip-flop had captured it. The tools slowed those paths down.
  • Over 1,400 top-level cells exist only for reset. All 1,262 top-level registers have an and2b in front that forces a 0 while rst is high, and a tree of 153 buffers fans the one rst pin out to all of them.
